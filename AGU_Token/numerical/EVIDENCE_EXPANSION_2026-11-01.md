# Evidence expansion for the AGU 2026 agentic software carbon study

Reviewed 1 November 2026. This is a source audit and scenario design, not a new end-to-end carbon estimate. The current seven-stage proxy model is described in [README](README.md), with the consolidated [reference register](REFERENCES.md). New token observations and the measured hardware benchmark do not share a workload or unit of analysis.

## 1. Developer work and daily task counts

The strongest company-internal workweek allocation found is [Kumar et al., *Time Warp*](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15287). It surveyed 484 Microsoft individual-contributor developers in the U.S. and India in June–July 2024, asking about hours in 16 activities in the preceding workweek. The response rate was 8.06%, so it is an informative company sample, not a representative census. The paper reports approximate mean shares: meetings/communication 12%, coding new features 11%, debugging 9%, architecture/design 6%, and PR/code review 5%. The other 11 activities account for the remaining **approximately** 57%. These values are percentages of all reported time and should not be renormalized to represent a complete agent-eligible task day.

`stage2_derived_day_example.csv` multiplies those reported shares by an **assumed eight-hour paid day**. It gives 0.88 h coding, 0.72 h debugging, 0.48 h design, 0.40 h review, 0.96 h meetings, and 4.56 h in other activities. These are illustrative daily equivalents of week-level averages, not observed day-level durations. If we retain the earlier **assumed** 8/2/20/2 human-hours-per-feature/debug/design/review task, the expected rates are 0.11/0.36/0.024/0.20 tasks per day. Fractional counts are sensible because tasks span days. The two-hour mean in [METR's randomized study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) describes 246 selected real repository issues worked by 16 experienced open-source developers; it is not a debugging-specific mean or an enterprise task distribution. [JetBrains' five-company review survey](https://kairos.jetbrains.com/research/articles/quo-vadis-code-review/) provides a separate review-time cross-check: median 3 h/week among 100 participating reviewers, IQR 2–6 h/week.

`stage2_duration_sensitivity.csv` makes the unmeasured task-duration span explicit. Its short/central/long **analyst scenarios** are debugging 0.5/2/8 h, feature coding 2/8/40 h, architecture 8/20/80 h, and PR review 0.25/2/8 h. With the derived daily hours above, these imply 1.44/0.36/0.09 debugging tasks per day, 0.44/0.11/0.022 feature tasks, 0.06/0.024/0.006 design tasks, and 1.60/0.20/0.05 reviews. The order is short/central/long task duration. These are **not confidence intervals or empirical quantiles**; they are a transparent range to replace with issue and time-diary data.

For the next Monte Carlo version, sample a full activity vector by role and company, then durations by activity and complexity. Do **not** turn 30% FTE into 30% of every category. Define `H_k` as paid hours/day in category `k`, `D_k` as human hours per accepted task, `a_k` as eligible share, `s_k` as agent success, and `r_k` as residual human review/correction fraction. Then `accepted_agent_tasks_k/day = (H_k / D_k) × a_k × s_k`; net substituted human hours/day are `accepted_agent_tasks_k × D_k × (1-r_k)`. The realized work share is the sum of net substituted hours divided by the chosen paid-day or technical-work denominator, **stated explicitly**. The 30/50/70% scenarios remain assumptions until `a_k`, `s_k`, and `r_k` have compatible measurements. An AI-assisted productivity change is not the same as autonomous task substitution.

## 2. Coding-agent token demand and enterprise reports

The [Standard Compute Agent Burn Index](https://standardcompute.com/agent-burn-index) gives provider-reported telemetry for 80,449 identified coding-agent requests from 14 September to 13 October 2026. Median request input is 99,730 tokens; 87% of input tokens are cache reads. Among 1,224 detected sessions, the median is 1.4 million total tokens. Among 303 active account-days, median total use is 8.6 million tokens and P90 is 87 million. These are **request, session, and active account-day** measures respectively. An account-day can contain multiple sessions and tasks, so the 8.6 million cannot be labeled tokens per accepted SWE task or tokens per engineer-day. The gateway sample is selected and request-weighted summaries favor busy accounts. It strongly motivates a high-usage scenario and separate cache classes.

[Uber's first-party report](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/efficient-software-factory/) says more than 70% of PRs were attributed to local or cloud agents and reports more than 30,000 agent skill executions per day. Its cost decomposition is `users × sessions/user × turns/session × requests/turn × tokens/request × price/token`. Uber does **not** report a numerical token-per-task statistic. PR attribution is not an FTE replacement fraction. [Meta's first-party report](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/04/16/developer-tools/capacity-efficiency-at-meta-how-unified-ai-agents-optimize-performance-at-hyperscale/) says a capacity investigation that took about 10 manual hours can take about 30 minutes with agents, but also supplies no token count. These reports extend the task taxonomy and deployment scale; neither can calibrate the token numerator.

[Salim et al., *Tokenomics*](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14470v1) analyze 30 ChatDev ProgramDev tasks run with a GPT-5 reasoning model. Review accounts for an average 59.4% of tokens, and the reported aggregate composition is 53.9% input, 24.4% output, 21.6% reasoning. Phase means are conditional on the phase occurring and must not be added as if every run executes every phase. Reasoning accounting differs across providers; confirm whether it is included in output tokens before summing categories. [Bai et al. v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22750v3) examine OpenHands on 500 SWE-bench Verified problems, four runs per model, eight models. They show large same-task variability, with up to 30-fold run differences for some problems and approximately twofold mean maximum/minimum ratios. This is a distribution-shape result, **not** a universal 30× correction factor.

The [Iternal commercial guide](https://iternal.ai/token-usage-guide) suggests million-token agent tasks, but provides no underlying trace sample or measurement procedure for its cited 1–3.5 million range. Use it to motivate data collection, not as a quantitative model input. The current local mini-SWE-agent traces may undercount some production workflows because they omit or reconstruct provider token categories, long-context replanning, tool loops, and multi-agent orchestration. Conversely, some external totals include cached context repeatedly and are not directly comparable to raw generated tokens. The direction and size of net bias are unmeasured.

The next token ledger must preserve a common unit: **accepted task**. For each attempt, save provider model, harness/version, input fresh, cache read, cache write, output visible, reasoning if separately reported, tool calls, retries, success, human review, and task family. A useful decomposition is `T_accepted = sum_over_attempts_and_calls(T_fresh + T_cache_read + T_cache_write + T_output + T_reasoning_if_exclusive)` / accepted tasks. `T_reasoning` must be omitted from the extra sum if the provider includes it in output. Compare agents only at a common acceptance and latency target. Routing/Jev savings must include router calls and any cache disruption.

## 3. Additional accelerators and large MoE models

`stage4_accelerator_catalog.csv` adds NVIDIA B200/B300/GB300, AMD MI300X/MI325X/MI350X/MI355X, and Google TPU v5p/v6e/7x to the measured A100/H100/H200 baseline. The catalog records vendor memory, bandwidth, and peak configurable board power where available. TPU per-chip power is left blank because the cited [Google Cloud TPU specification](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x) does not provide a comparable public value. None of these vendor specifications is a measured joules-per-token result. [NVIDIA B200](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/documents/HGX-B200-PCF-Summary.pdf), [NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra](https://dam-cdn.nvd.orangelogic.com/AssetLink/1k0p832eq8r5ca0u5383ie5o4tp3bst1.pdf), and [AMD MI355X](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html) illustrate how peak power and memory change with configuration.

`stage4_moe_model_catalog.csv` adds public MoE examples: [gpt-oss-120b](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/419b6906-9da6-406c-a19d-1bb078ac7637/oai_gpt-oss_model_card.pdf) 116.8B total/5.1B active parameters, [Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct) 480B/35B, [DeepSeek-V3](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3) 671B/37B, and [Kimi K2](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K2-Instruct) 1T/32B. Active parameters affect arithmetic per generated token, but the full model's weights, quantization, KV cache, expert placement, and interconnect constrain GPU count and throughput. Scaling the measured 30B-A3B benchmark by total parameters **or** active parameters alone would be physically unsound. The next benchmark matrix must replay matched agent traces across model × hardware × context × concurrency, measure power and throughput together, and impose a latency limit.

## 4. U.S. data-center electricity supply

The [IEA *Energy and AI* report](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-supply-for-ai) provides a U.S.-specific **physical** data-center supply mix for 2024: natural gas over 40%, renewables 24%, nuclear around 20%, coal around 15%. It includes local grids and on-site generation, not contractual purchases. These are rounded verbal shares, so they do not constitute an exact four-category probability vector.

[Guidi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05420v1) estimate electricity attribution for 403 U.S. hyperscale data centers from May 2024 to April 2025 using balancing-authority generation shares. They report natural gas 38.2%, coal 15.2%, nuclear 20.9%, renewables 25.3%, and a residual fossil component. Their electricity-weighted carbon intensity is 0.545 kg **CO2**/kWh, compared with 0.370 kg CO2/kWh U.S. national average in their method. This is a preprint, a facility sample, an annual attributional allocation, and **CO2 rather than CO2e**. It is a valuable data-center-specific sensitivity case, not a universal factor for an arbitrary U.S. facility.

The primary operational calculation should still use the actual site and corresponding [EPA eGRID subregion CO2e factor](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/summary-data) when location is known. If unknown, present both IEA/Guidi data-center mix context and a national-grid fallback, keeping boundaries and years explicit. Do not draw a discrete fuel type for every GPU request: physically a data center draws power from a grid and its mix is an accounting allocation. Do not multiply Guidi's 0.545 kg CO2/kWh by eGRID CO2e or IPCC life-cycle factors. Marginal emissions for **additional** data-center load require a separate location/time-specific scenario.

## Next calculation gate

Before updating the fan and company carbon numbers, collect paired observations of accepted work, full token traces, and matched hardware energy. The immediate low-cost sensitivity analysis can vary task-day assumptions and token/day distributions **without presenting resulting kWh as measured**. A numerical energy extension needs at least one full replay of an agent-shaped input/output/cache sequence on B200 or MI300X, a large MoE model, measured board plus host power, and a site-specific grid factor. Until then the existing end-to-end numbers remain an illustrative calculation check.
