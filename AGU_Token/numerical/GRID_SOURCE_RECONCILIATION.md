# Step 6 source and emissions reconciliation

Checked 1 November 2026. This audit accompanies the [grid-source probability table](stage6_grid_source_probabilities.csv) and [reference register](REFERENCES.md). It uses [EPA eGRID2023 Revision 2 detailed data](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/detailed-data), [Guidi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05420v1), [EIA Table 3.1.B](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/annual/table.php?t=epa_03_01_b.html), and [IPCC AR5 Annex III, Table A.III.2](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2018/02/ipcc_wg3_ar5_annex-iii.pdf). All values are U.S. data or U.S. source shares paired with the IPCC's cross-technology global life-cycle literature ranges. The working mix is **not** a measurement of any particular data center's electricity procurement.

## Fuel weights and factors

Guidi's 403-hyperscale-facility annual generation attribution reports gas 38.2%, coal 15.2%, nuclear 20.9%, and renewables 25.3%. The 0.4-point residual is assigned to “other fossil” and given a gas life-cycle factor proxy. The Guidi source shares are rounded, and its separate fossil subtotal of 53.9% is 0.1 point above gas + coal + this residual. The renewable 25.3% is allocated across wind, solar, conventional hydro, biomass, and geothermal using EIA's **national** 2024 all-sector utility-scale proportions. This fills missing detail as a scenario assumption; the 403 facilities' true renewable submix was not published in the cited text.

EIA released final 2025 generation data on **21 October 2026**. We added them in [one two-year source table](stage6_renewable_split_eia_2024_2025.csv) but retain 2024 for the central mix because it overlaps more of Guidi's May 2024–April 2025 window. Using the 2025 renewable split instead gives central-share IPCC low/median/high factors of **0.275046 / 0.324917 / 0.562242 kg CO₂e/kWh**, versus **0.275009 / 0.324738 / 0.572841** with 2024. The median changes by **+0.000179 kg CO₂e/kWh**; the high envelope changes more because 2025 hydro's renewable share is smaller. EIA's published totals differ by 1 thousand MWh from the sum of rounded source components in each year, so the code normalizes the component sums. [The paired year-and-κ sensitivity table](model_outputs/grid_mix_kappa_sensitivity.csv) uses identical coarse source-share random draws for the two proxy years.

IPCC's technology-specific low/median/high **life-cycle CO₂e** values produce a central-share weighted factor of **0.275 / 0.325 / 0.573 kg CO₂e/kWh**. Table A.III.2's hydropower maximum is 2.2 kg CO₂e/kWh for a reservoir-specific extreme. The EIA solar category includes a small solar-thermal component while the factor is utility PV; EIA biomass is a mixed category while the factor is dedicated biomass. These are explicit technology proxies. The three columns are literature envelopes, not P10/P50/P90. The model samples only uncertainty in the **shares** through a Dirichlet scenario, with assumed concentration κ=100; see [κ sensitivity](model_outputs/grid_mix_kappa_sensitivity.csv).

For an **operational** comparison, EPA eGRID U.S. national total is **0.349667 kg CO₂e/kWh**. In the same official workbook, national fuel-specific output rates are gas **0.408246**, coal **1.018108**, and oil **0.707893 kg CO₂e/kWh**. The exact workbook sheets, field codes and unit conversions are in [the eGRID reconciliation CSV](stage6_egrid_reconciliation.csv). Applying only these three U.S. fuel rates to Guidi's gas, coal and residual-other weights (oil used as a rough residual proxy) gives **0.314 kg CO₂e/kWh before non-fossil operational contributions**. This is an exploratory cross-check, not a new calibrated data-center factor.

## Unresolved discrepancy

Guidi report **0.545 kg CO₂/kWh** for their 403-facility sample and about **0.535 kg CO₂/kWh** for PJM. EPA eGRID2023 Revision 2's **PJM balancing-authority** total output rate in sheet `BA23`, field `BAC2ERTA`, is **719.405 lb CO₂e/MWh = 0.326317 kg CO₂e/kWh**. The CO₂ versus CO₂e distinction cannot by itself explain this roughly 0.21 kg/kWh gap. Guidi's paper may be using a different plant set, location weighting or another transformation; the published text and aggregate tables inspected here do not settle it. **The paper's reported carbon intensity is not used to calibrate the model.** Before claiming a facility-level U.S. footprint, obtain the underlying plant/facility allocation or author clarification and reconcile it with eGRID.

These checks do not make the eGRID U.S. average an appropriate factor for a specified data-center site. [EPA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/using-egrid-to-determine-emissions.pdf) recommends the site's **eGRID subregion total-output rate** for an electricity-use footprint; the U.S. total is a fallback because site locations are not supplied. Additional-load consequences require a separately documented marginal grid model. [IEA's physical data-center supply mix](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-supply-for-ai) is a qualitative cross-check; its rounded U.S. shares are not normalized into a second exact probability vector.
