# Evidence and reference register

Checked 1 November 2026. The CSV ledgers contain row-level URLs; this register identifies the exact claim used in the seven-stage calculation. A source that describes a related population is a comparison, not a calibration observation.

| Stage | Primary reference and locator | Quantity supported | Use and limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2, engineer time | [Kumar et al., *Time Warp*, Microsoft developer survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15287), activity table | Reported weekly time shares in coding, debugging, design, review | Four category shares are an observed but selected sample. The 8-hour daily normalization and 30/50/70% attempted delegation are analyst scenarios. |
| 1, autonomy | [Microsoft, *You Shall Not Pass*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/you-shall-not-pass-where-and-why-developers-draw-the-line-on-ai-autonomy/) and [METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) | Task-specific acceptance and benchmark task-duration horizon | Neither measures autonomous FTE displacement. |
| 2, task duration | [METR randomized developer study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) | Selected repository-issue human time | Plausibility check only; task-family duration ranges remain assumed. |
| 3, agent demand | [Bai et al., agent-token study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750) and [Salim et al., *Tokenomics*](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14470v1) | Agent trace variability and input/reasoning/review composition | Different scaffolds; no direct multiplier applied. Local attempt counts come from [Experiment 1](../../Experiment/exp1_summary.md). |
| 3, production demand | [Standard Compute Agent Burn Index](https://standardcompute.com/agent-burn-index) | Request, session, and account-day token distributions | Units differ from an accepted software task; only separate stress scenarios use these values. |
| 4, measured service | [Local Experiment 2 protocol](../../Experiment/exp2_token_gen/README.md) and [raw results](../../Experiment/exp2_results/exp2_raw.csv) | A100/H100/H200 benchmark throughput and board power | Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B with specified prompt, output, and concurrency; no inference claim for untested GPUs or closed models. |
| 5, facility overhead | [DOE/FEMP data center design guide](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-07/best-practice-guide-data-center-design.pdf) and [LBNL/DOE 2025 U.S. data center update](https://www.energy.gov/documents/united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report-2025-update), Figure 9 | PUE definition; updated estimated 2024 national average PUE 1.45 | The model's triangular 1.2/1.4/1.6 PUE remains an analyst scenario, not a fitted site distribution. LBNL also estimates 192 TWh U.S. data-center electricity use in 2024; that is national context, not this project's per-token measurement. |
| 6, U.S. data-center supply | [Guidi et al. 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05420v1), electricity-source section and Figure 4 | Attributional shares for 403 U.S. hyperscale centers in May 2024–April 2025 | Four rounded broad shares define the central probability vector; the 0.4% residual and renewable submix are analyst proxies. Not a census or contractual procurement mix. |
| 6, independent context | [IEA, *Energy and AI* (2025)](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-supply-for-ai), Regional outlook | U.S. physical data-center supply: gas >40%, renewables 24%, nuclear ~20%, coal ~15% | Rounded verbal shares support plausibility, not an exact probability vector. |
| 6, renewable submix | [EIA Electric Power Annual Table 3.1.B](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/annual/table.php?t=epa_03_01_b.html), annual totals for 2024 and 2025 | U.S. all-sector utility-scale generation by renewable source | 2024 is the main proxy because it overlaps the Guidi study period; final 2025 data released 21 October 2026 are a temporal sensitivity. EIA aggregates can differ by 1 thousand MWh from summed rounded components. Neither year measures HDC-specific renewable supply. |
| 6–7, source factors | [IPCC AR5 WGIII Annex III, Table A.III.2](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2018/02/ipcc_wg3_ar5_annex-iii.pdf) | Technology-specific life-cycle min/median/max in g CO₂e/kWh | Converted to kg by dividing by 1,000. Bounds are literature extrema, not probability quantiles. Biomass is dedicated-biomass proxy; solar PV stands in for the small EIA solar-thermal component; hydro maximum is reservoir-specific. |
| 7, operational benchmark | [EPA eGRID2023 Revision 2 summary](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/summary-data) and [detailed workbook](https://www.epa.gov/egrid/detailed-data) | U.S. total 770.884 lb CO₂e/MWh; fuel and balancing-authority rates in [reconciliation CSV](stage6_egrid_reconciliation.csv) | Multiply by 0.45359237/1000 to get kg CO₂e/kWh. This is annual-average operational generation, a different boundary from IPCC life-cycle. |
| 7, factor choice | [EPA, *Using eGRID to Determine Emissions from Electricity Use*](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-01/using-egrid-to-determine-emissions.pdf), steps 1–3 | EPA recommends subregion total-output factors for electricity-use footprints | U.S. total is a fallback when site unknown. Nonbaseload and hourly marginal factors answer a different, consequential question. |

## Reproducibility of the grid stage

The 2024 central mix and nine IPCC factors are in [the probability table](stage6_grid_source_probabilities.csv); original EIA category values for both years are in [the renewable split table](stage6_renewable_split_eia_2024_2025.csv). The model and share sensitivity are in [grid_mix.py](grid_mix.py) and [the sensitivity output](model_outputs/grid_mix_kappa_sensitivity.csv). The detailed [source reconciliation](GRID_SOURCE_RECONCILIATION.md) records the unresolved difference between Guidi's reported intensity and the EPA workbook; the model does not force those quantities to agree.
