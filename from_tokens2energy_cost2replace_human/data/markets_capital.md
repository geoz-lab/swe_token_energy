## Investment Thesis Summaries

### Nuclear Power

Nuclear is emerging as the preferred baseload power source for hyperscalers seeking 24/7 carbon-free electricity. The Microsoft–Constellation TMI-1 deal (~$100–$112/MWh estimated, 20-year term, 835 MW) and Amazon's 1,920 MW PPA with Talen Energy have established market-clearing prices well above typical wholesale grid rates (~$40–60/MWh). Uranium benefited from this narrative, spiking to $106/lb in January 2024 from $32.50/lb in 2020, though it has since pulled back to ~$73/lb by year-end 2024. SMR commitments (Amazon Energy Northwest: 320–960 MW, ~2030 delivery) represent a long-dated option on the nuclear supply constraint.

### Data Center REITs and Developers

Equinix ($8.7B revenue, 242 facilities, 365 MW xScale leased at ~$6B total contract value) and Digital Realty (>300 centers, 9.1M sq ft under construction, record $100M Q4 2024 bookings) are the dominant publicly traded platforms. The investment thesis rests on structural supply/demand imbalance: power availability constraints (grid interconnection, permitting) suppress supply while AI-driven demand grows at 30%/year (IEA). REITs benefit from long-term leases (10–20 years) with escalators. Key risk: valuations embed premium AI-demand expectations; any hyperscaler capex slowdown would compress multiples sharply.

### Equipment Suppliers (Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric)

Vertiv is the clearest financial proxy for AI data center buildout: $8.0B revenue (+17% YoY), Q4 +26% YoY, organic orders +30% trailing 12 months. As GPU rack densities escalate from ~40 kW/rack (air-cooled H100) to 120+ kW/rack (GB200 NVL72), power delivery and thermal management equipment become critical bottlenecks. This shift — more power density per rack, not just more racks — is a multi-year capex cycle for UPS systems, liquid cooling distribution units, and busways. Eaton and Schneider Electric compete in the same space; all three benefit from the IEA's projected 30%/year AI server electricity growth through 2030.

### Power Futures and Uranium

The hyperscaler nuclear PPA market has established a price floor for firm 24/7 clean power at $100–$112/MWh — roughly 2–2.5× typical wholesale power prices. This creates a structural long position in nuclear generation assets and uranium. Uranium at $73/lb (year-end 2024) has corrected from the $106/lb January 2024 peak but remains +125% from $32.50/lb in 2020. Power futures in constrained grids (ERCOT, PJM) are the shorter-dated instrument: ERCOT load growth of +7% (2025) and +14% (2026), with 233 GW of interconnection requests, points to sustained price pressure through 2027+.

### Copper

Data center copper demand is significant and growing, though estimates conflict widely (see conflict note above). Current global refined copper deficit is already ~304,000 tonnes (2025, Wood Mackenzie). S&P Global projects global copper demand growing from 28M to 42M tonnes by 2040 (+50%), with a ~6M tonne shortfall projected by 2035. The AI copper story is real but is one component of a broader electrification thesis (EVs, grid modernization); investors should not isolate data centers as the sole driver.

## Scenario Analysis: Incremental Demand from Engineering Substitution

These figures represent the power and capital needed *specifically for engineering-FTE substitution workloads* — not total data center growth, which is driven by the full spectrum of AI use cases.

| Substitution | SW Eng. FTEs Replaced | Additional Power Demand | Data Center Capex | Annual Energy Cost | Annual Labor Savings |
|-------------|----------------------|------------------------|-------------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| 10% | 2.4 million | 36.8 MW (0.037 GW) | ~$552 million | ~$19 million/yr | ~$170 billion/yr |
| **30%** | **7.2 million** | **110 MW (0.11 GW)** | **~$1.65 billion** | **~$58 million/yr** | **~$511 billion/yr** |
| 80% | 19.2 million | 293 MW (0.29 GW) | ~$4.40 billion | ~$154 million/yr | ~$1.36 trillion/yr |

> **Economic case is overwhelmingly positive at all scenarios.** Energy cost is a rounding error relative to labor displacement value. The binding constraints are AI capability (SWE-bench at 13.86% for Devin 2.0), institutional adoption speed, and regulatory/liability considerations — not infrastructure cost.
